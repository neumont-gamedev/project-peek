# Sprint Retrospective Rubric

**Total Points: 10**

The Sprint Retrospective evaluates a student's ability to reflect on their Capstone project progress, identify challenges, assess their sprint experience, and establish actionable improvements for the next sprint.

## 1. What Went Well (3 Points)

**Full Marks — 3 points**
- Clearly identifies accomplishments, successes, and effective practices from the previous sprint.
- Demonstrates thoughtful reflection on what contributed to progress.

**No Marks — 0 points**

## 2. What Could Be Improved (3 Points)

**Full Marks — 3 points**
- Identifies specific challenges, setbacks, or areas for improvement.
- Demonstrates an understanding of what caused difficulties and how they could be addressed.

**No Marks — 0 points**

## 3. Sprint Experience Rating (1 Point)

**Full Marks — 1 point**
- Selects a rating that reflects the student's overall experience and progress during the sprint.

**No Marks — 0 points**

## 4. Action Items for Next Sprint (3 Points)

**Full Marks — 3 points**
- Establishes specific, realistic, and actionable improvements that can be implemented and evaluated during the next sprint.

**No Marks — 0 points**

---

## Scoring Summary

| Criterion | Maximum Points |
|---|---:|
| What Went Well | 3 |
| What Could Be Improved | 3 |
| Sprint Experience Rating | 1 |
| Action Items for Next Sprint | 3 |
| **Total** | **10** |

## Grading Guidelines for AI

When evaluating a student's Sprint Retrospective:

1. Evaluate each criterion independently using the descriptions above.
2. Assign scores based on the rubric's defined performance levels.
3. Use only the following scores for each criterion:
   - What Went Well: 3 or 0
   - What Could Be Improved: 3 or 0
   - Sprint Experience Rating: 1 or 0
   - Action Items for Next Sprint: 3 or 0
4. Award full points when the student adequately meets the criterion's requirements.
5. Do not deduct points simply because the student experienced difficulties, failed to complete sprint goals, or gave their sprint a low rating. Evaluate the quality of their reflection, not just their project outcomes.
6. Provide a brief explanation for any criterion that does not receive full points.
7. Do not assume missing information or invent student responses.
8. Calculate the final score out of 10 points.

**Important:** The rubric uses binary scoring (full marks or no marks). Do not assign partial credit unless the instructor explicitly authorizes it.
